# Smart Street Lighting System with Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting

## Abstract

The development of **energy-efficient urban infrastructure** has become increasingly important due to rising energy costs and growing environmental concerns. This project presents the **design and implementation of a smart street lighting system** that optimizes energy consumption through **real-time motion detection** and **ambient light sensing**.

The system integrates **piezoelectric tiles** as an energy-harvesting mechanism, converting mechanical energy generated by pedestrian footsteps into electrical energy. Experimental measurements produced an output of **5.42 V** and **0.49 mA**. While the measured voltage demonstrates potential for energy harvesting applications, the relatively low current output presents a limitation for direct battery-charging applications.

Overall, the results demonstrate the potential of integrating **renewable energy technologies into public infrastructure**. However, further improvements are necessary to increase current generation and optimize energy storage. Future research may focus on improving the efficiency of the energy-harvesting mechanism and evaluating the system's scalability for larger urban applications.

---

## Background of the Project

The project aims to develop a **smart street lighting system** that reduces energy consumption by dynamically adjusting the brightness of streetlights based on **real-time pedestrian movement** and **ambient light conditions**.

The system incorporates **piezoelectric tiles**, which convert the mechanical energy generated by pedestrian footsteps into electrical energy. The harvested energy can then be stored and utilized to support the operation of the streetlights, reducing dependence on conventional power sources.

By combining **smart lighting control** with **renewable energy harvesting**, the proposed system provides a sustainable approach to urban street lighting. The project demonstrates how energy-efficient technologies can be integrated into public infrastructure to promote **energy conservation, sustainability, and smarter urban environments**.

---

## Key Features

* **Smart Lighting Control** – Automatically adjusts streetlight brightness based on detected conditions.
* **Motion Detection** – Detects pedestrian movement to control lighting intensity.
* **Ambient Light Detection** – Adjusts lighting according to surrounding light levels.
* **Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting** – Converts pedestrian footsteps into electrical energy.
* **Energy Storage** – Stores harvested electrical energy for potential use in powering the system.
* **Energy-Efficient Design** – Reduces reliance on conventional energy sources.

## Conclusion

This study confirms the feasibility of a piezoelectric-powered smart street lighting system as an energy-efficient solution for urban infrastructure. By harnessing mechanical energy from pedestrian footsteps and dynamically adjusting illumination based on real-time conditions, the
system minimizes energy consumption while maintaining adequate lighting. The voltage output
of 5.42 V aligns well with low-energy requirements, but the low current output of 0.49 mA
presents a challenge for practical battery charging. To address this, further optimization of the
energy harvesting circuit and enhancements in energy storage are necessary. The study
advocates for wider application of piezoelectric energy harvesting in public infrastructure, with
future work focused on improving current output and exploring long-term performance.
